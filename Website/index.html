Inverted Pendulum Control System

They thought they could do three...

Overview

Earlier this year (2026), my friend shared with me an Instagram reel of a triple inverted pendulum balancing itself on a cart. It included the equations of motion for the system, a massive bulk of math that neither of us could come close to comprehending, but it seemed like it might be interesting to work towards. So, over the last couple of months, he and I, along with a couple other friends, have been doing exactly that: working on solving a single inverted pendulum, a classic control systems problem, as a stepping stone toward eventually solving the triple inverted pendulum. This website documents our work, showing exactly what not to do to get a pendulum on a cart upright.

Technical Details

To balance our inverted pendulum, we used an energy-based swing-up controller to bring the pendulum close to the upright position, where the LQR controller would take over. We will explain how LQR and LQG works later in this section, but if you want more information about control systems with much better explanations than we can provide, we recommend this youtube course, by Dr. Brunton at the University of Washington. This video series was a huge help to us when programming everything on the MATLAB side, and his entire playlist is very digestible and fun to follow. If you ever want to get into control systems, this is the playlist to start with in our opinion.

Now, starting the process, we first need to derive the equations of motion for our system. This can get a little complicated, and could potentially require a much higher level of math knowledge, but you can also find the equations of motion through some free body diagrams and algebra. We will show both approaches here, so feel free to expand whichever method is more comfortable for you. The Lagrangian approach is more elegant, but the Newtonian approach is more accessible for anyone with a basic understanding of physics.

Lagrangian Approach:
  • Define the kinetic and potential energy of the system.
  • Use the Lagrangian, which is the difference between kinetic and potential energy, to derive the equations of motion.
  • Apply the Euler-Lagrange equation to obtain the equations of motion.

Newtonian Approach:
  • Draw free body diagrams for the cart and pendulum.
  • Apply Newton's second law (F=ma) to each component of the system.
  • Combine the equations to derive the equations of motion.
